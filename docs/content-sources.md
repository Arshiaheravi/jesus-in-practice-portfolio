# Content sources and editorial decisions

Initial editorial research: 16 October 2026. Implementation boundary updated 17 October 2026. This records source attribution, the content approach, and outstanding launch review; it is not legal advice or a theological endorsement.

## Shared library

`packages/content` contains 24 original practical reflections: 12 linked to the King James Version of the Bible and 12 linked to the Book of Mormon. Website and dashboard share this bundled baseline through `@jesus-in-practice/content`. The live member catalogue can additionally load eligible human-reviewed publications from the backend; no real imported edition or added AI publication is supplied with this implementation. Each lesson has a stable URL slug, source collection, precise passage reference, original summary, plain-language explanation, everyday example, suggested action, concise message copy, reflection question, category, estimated practice time, and source URL. These are selected practices, not a complete catalogue or systematic summary of either work. Keep bundled IDs stable; imported drafts cannot replace those reserved IDs.

The source links point to the official scripture library of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Bible links identify the KJV text in that library; the linked site also contains denominational study notes. Our app does not reproduce those notes, chapter headings, photographs, illustrations, or other site assets. Each passage's chapter page was opened during research. The references and central concepts were checked against the passages; no pastoral, academic, or professional editorial review is claimed. A source link is attribution, not evidence of an affiliation or endorsement.

Display summaries as an **original reflection** or **in practice**. Do not present them as verbatim scripture, as a new translation, or as an authoritative explanation of what all Christians believe. The action and reflection question are application suggestions from this product. Time estimates describe the proposed action, not a promise that reading or practising a lesson will take exactly that time.

## Practical explanation and message format

The expanded format answers four questions: Where does this idea come from? What does it mean in ordinary language? What might it look like in someone's day? What is one thing to try?

| Field                    | Editorial purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `reference` and `source` | Identify the exact passage and collection; retain the primary link for context.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `summary`                | Briefly connect the passage with its setting or central teaching.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `plainMeaning`           | Explain the practical idea in no more than two sentences, without assuming familiarity with religious vocabulary.                                                                                                                                |
| `everydayExample`        | Illustrate a recognizable situation in no more than two sentences. These are invented teaching scenarios, not testimonials, claims about the reader, or events attributed to scripture.                                                          |
| `action`                 | Give one manageable next step, with permission, capacity, and safety boundaries where relevant.                                                                                                                                                  |
| `smsText`                | Provide concise original copy containing the collection, exact reference, simple meaning, and a realistic example/action. It is not a scripture quotation; the server adds service text and quotes the final encoded payload before a live send. |

The 24 messages use situations such as carrying groceries, missing an email, sharing housework, a delayed checkout, and making room for a new coworker. They do not promise a result from completing the action. Explain concepts such as humility, stewardship, and charity in everyday language while retaining the passage's religious context; do not turn every passage into a generic productivity slogan.

The bundled library's editorial limit for `smsText` is 360 characters before service-specific sender or unsubscribe text. Imported editorial drafts have their own bounded schema, documented in [content pipeline](content-pipeline.md). Neither limit guarantees one SMS segment: encoding and concatenation affect the transmitted segment count, as illustrated by [Twilio's official character-limit documentation](https://www.twilio.com/docs/glossary/what-sms-character-limit). Exact references retain their range punctuation. The implemented server renders sender/unsubscribe text, counts the final encoded payload, freezes the quote at the configured CAD rate per segment, and rejects messages above its ten-segment cap. This describes code behavior, not a verified provider delivery or cost.

During the expanded-content review, several distinctions were checked again against the primary passages:

- [Matthew 6:3–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/6?lang=eng&id=p3-p4#p3) concerns giving to people in need without seeking public recognition. The example now uses a practical gift rather than implying that the passage directly describes household tidying.
- [Alma 37:6–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/37?lang=eng&id=p6-p7#p6) occurs in instruction about caring for sacred records. Beginning a task with a small step is our application; the text is not a promise of productivity or success.
- [Alma 37:37](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/37?lang=eng&id=p37#p37) mentions trust at night and thanks in the morning. An evening gratitude exercise is explicitly our suggested adaptation.
- [Ether 12:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/ether/12?lang=eng&id=p27#p27) connects humility with faith and grace. Asking for help is an application, not a guarantee that effort will eliminate every limitation.
- [Mosiah 4:24–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/4?lang=eng&id=p24-p27#p24) recognizes limited resources and strength alongside care for others. The examples protect essential needs and realistic capacity.
- [3 Nephi 13:19–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/13?lang=eng&id=p19-p21#p19) concerns earthly and heavenly treasure. Attention to relationships is a suggested everyday application, not a replacement definition of heavenly treasure.

See [AI-assisted content design](ai-content-design.md) for the editorial rationale and current limits. The gated corpus importer, bounded AI-assisted drafting adapter, human review/publication, and live catalogue are implemented; no full-text source artifact is included and no real AI call or imported edition has been verified. Only approved, currently permitted published versions can extend the bundled delivery pool.

## Scope and care

- Bible is the initial collection. Book of Mormon or both collections are explicit user choices. Keep every lesson's source visible in cards, detail pages, and message previews.
- The Word of Wisdom is in [Doctrine and Covenants 89](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/89?lang=eng), not the Book of Mormon. It is outside the current two-collection library. Adding it requires a separately named collection and editorial scope decision.
- Forgiveness does not require unsafe contact, excusing abuse, or instant reconciliation. Advice about generosity respects limited resources; grief content avoids promising to fix someone's pain. Do not infer spiritual failure from illness, poverty, difficulty, or a missed practice.
- These reflections provide invitations to everyday practice, not medical, mental-health, financial, or legal advice. They do not promise specific wellbeing outcomes, healing, wealth, salvation, or guaranteed personal change.
- No invented testimonials, subscriber counts, professional qualifications, customer outcomes, or church endorsements are used. Before launch, arrange editorial review by people familiar with the selected traditions and the intended audience.

## Rights and publication

The bundled app library uses original summaries and actions with references and external links. No verse text is copied into those 24 practices. This avoids depending on a licence to reproduce a modern scripture site's presentation, but is not a blanket legal clearance for editions, quotations, images, or commercial use.

Imported editions require a manifest identifying the exact English KJV or Book of Mormon text family, provenance, normalization decisions, artifact checksum, and independently established chapter/verse inventory. Rights default off and are recorded per edition, territory, use and expiry. Storage, retrieval, AI processing, display, email and SMS are separate uses; a download or public-domain label is not automatic approval. The importer accepts a bounded local normalized artifact and does not scrape the church website. [Content pipeline](content-pipeline.md) records researched eBible and Project Gutenberg candidates, their limitations, CLI commands, and technical checks. None is an approved Canadian commercial corpus in this repository.

Imported lesson versions retain their passage key, edition title/checksum, drafting provenance and human review events. Review checks source context, meaning, practical safety, rights and the distinction between original explanation and quotation. Withdrawal and rights changes remove publications from eligibility, and a queued message's version is checked again before submission. See [operator guide](operator-guide.md). A role assignment and automated schema test do not establish an editor's qualifications or theological correctness.

The Church's [Terms of Use](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/learn/legal/terms-of-use/go?lang=eng) distinguish permitted personal noncommercial use from other uses that require permission, and identify protected trademarks. Do not assume that material found on the site is reusable in a paid app. Obtain any necessary permission before copying its protected content or using its marks as branding. The product should describe itself as independent.

Do not assume the KJV has the same legal status in every market or that an underlying historical text clears a modern edition's additions. [Cambridge's permissions page](https://www.cambridge.org/gp/rights-and-permissions/permissions) routes Bible permissions to its specialist team; its [KJV publication notice](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805212/28626/copyright/9780521228626_copyright_info.pdf) describes Crown rights administered in the United Kingdom. Confirm the edition, territory, format, and commercial rights before adding substantial verbatim scripture. The linked specialist permissions page returned HTTP 403 during this research, so its current detailed terms were not independently inspected.

## Canadian messaging and privacy considerations

Default preview mode does not send email or SMS, create accounts, take payments, or record production consent. It clearly identifies local settings and simulated examples. Live-mode server implementations now cover authenticated accounts, consent, messaging and prepaid SMS; they remain separately gated pending actual operator/provider setup and verification. Use fictitious or non-sensitive data in preview and test fixtures.

For a future Canadian launch, the [CRTC's CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm) says commercial electronic messages, including relevant SMS, require consent, sender identification, and an unsubscribe mechanism. It describes readily performed unsubscribe options, including SMS STOP, and a deadline no later than 10 business days for processing requests. Treat legal classification and any exceptions as launch review matters; do not assume religious subject matter makes a paid service exempt.

Implemented design decisions include separate unchecked email and SMS permissions, channel-specific preferences, clear withdrawal/pause controls, verified destinations, and server-retained consent/version evidence. Account creation, verification, terms acceptance and purchasing credit do not grant recurring-message consent. SMS permission is tied to the displayed rate per segment; a changed rate or wording requires a fresh grant. The configured operator identity is displayed beside consent controls and on policy/support pages; missing identity prevents new grants. The real legal name/address and approved policies remain owner inputs and must not be fabricated. Suppression is enforced server-side, including signed provider complaints/hard bounces and STOP; a browser preference is not sufficient. See [backend API](backend-api.md), [financial operations](financial-operations.md) and [production operations](production-operations.md).

The [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada's meaningful-consent guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/appropriate-handling-of-personal-information/collecting-personal-information-and-consent/consent/gl_omc_201805/) identifies religious or philosophical beliefs among generally sensitive information and calls for understandable, specific consent and appropriate collection. Source preferences and private reflections may reveal such information. Minimize what is stored, explain any browser storage clearly, provide deletion/export controls when applicable, and do not enable tracking or transmit reflections to providers by default. Before live collection, review applicable federal and provincial obligations, service-provider locations, retention, access controls, and the intended age audience with qualified advice.

## Maintenance checks

- Run `pnpm --filter @jesus-in-practice/content test` when changing content. The checks cover URL-safe unique IDs, preservation of existing IDs, required fields, short explanation/example length, exact message attribution, editorial message length, category coverage, source-link classification, balanced collection coverage, and boundaries in forgiveness content. They do not establish theological or legal correctness, assess the quality of a scenario, or validate SMS delivery.
- Review the full passage in context whenever a reference, summary, or action changes. Verify collection labels and source links, including redirects, periodically and before publication.
- Keep source choices consistent across onboarding, library filters, daily practice, and message previews. A Bible-only setting must not silently include Book of Mormon lessons.
- For the imported collection, run the backend content tests and validate the exact edition/artifact before changing publication or rights. Structural, synthetic-fixture and mocked-AI tests do not verify a real corpus or a live model's editorial accuracy.
- Review legal text and message consent wording against actual providers, sender identity, operational policies and data flows. Implemented controls do not establish live-service legal compliance or provider success.
